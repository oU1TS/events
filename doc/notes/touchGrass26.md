## The Story & Context
We from the oU1TS community attended Touch Grass on behalf of our newly built initiative, Penguins_Cafe_UITS. A week earlier (November 14, 2026), I had sent a formal collaboration proposal to The Penguins Club (TPC) to establish a recurring partnership—bringing TPC members in to run FOSS and Linux sessions at UITS and guide our juniors in their community activity. 

![](https://res.cloudinary.com/b1tranger/image/upload/v1795713558/proposal_wy8280.webp)

While formal university clubs and wings (like the UITS Cyber Security Wing, coordinated with Reyazul Islam @!LUZAYER, CSE-54, alongside TPC representative Zobayer Hasan @heySolus, CSE-51) handle major institutional tracks, Penguins Cafe was launched as a flexible, newcomer-friendly space for day-to-day peer learning. For a growing ecosystem like oU1TS, external collaborations like this bring in fresh expertise, mentorship, and pathways into the broader Bangladeshi open-source scene.

The afternoon started casually—sitting on the grass, catching up on mundane things about people and community efforts. Fazle Rabbi, the admin of TPC, shared his thoughts on FOSS and his experiences leading TPC over the years. He noted how much harder it has become nowadays to catch up regularly due to everyone's busy schedules, yet he kept coming back to a simple truth about real communities: "Wherever you go, you should be able to seek people out, and people who are nearby will show up." Throughout the conversation, he would joke about the realities of society and describe his peers through "Penguinified" metaphors—which explains why [Penguinify](https://hd189b.space/penguinify/) exists.

As more people arrived, Fazle bhai turned the spotlight toward me and asked about my vision for oU1TS and how it came about. He seemed genuinely interested, so I shared the origin story: what started as a simple resource-sharing group gradually scaled into a broader community. I had to create the parent initiative oU1TS (a wordplay on "UITS" with an "o" prefix standing for an online-based community) simply to bring multiple specialized initiatives together under one unified umbrella. 

![](../files/oU1TS.webp)

At its core, the reason was straightforward—we lacked a proper community, so we started with a simple idea and built from there. Because almost all projects under oU1TS are open-sourced, the philosophy clicked immediately with TPC, who operate on the ethos of being "Fuelled by Free and Open-Source Software (FOSS) and the Linux philosophy" ([thepenguins.club](https://thepenguins.club/)).

Both Fazle Rabbi and Infinite went on to share their own experiences leading active student tech communities at BRACU and SUST, respectively. Hearing their stories was deeply reassuring—it felt good to know that the time and effort I have been pouring into the people around oU1TS wasn't in vain.

Soon after, others joined in alongside Utsob bhai and @bdeshi, and the party truly kicked off. One detail that stood out about @bdeshi bhai was his background with OSSU—Fazle bhai introduced him as an OSSU graduate, which added real weight to his presence and his Why FOSS? session. In a lighthearted yet telling moment, Rabbi bhai also hinted on the decision for the next person to lead TPC.

Then there was Tanjim bhai, easily the nerdiest of the bunch, who had a sharp, thoughtful remark on virtually every topic that came up. When asked what he thought about our university student portal getting hacked, his reply was instant and deadpan: "So what?" I am still processing some of the angles from those conversations, but the bigger takeaway felt clear: in the grand scheme of things, real progress comes down to trial and error—learning from past mistakes and communicating well to see things through. Later, @bdeshi bhai pulled out his massive collection of open-source and tech stickers, and everyone lined up to pick their favorites—while Tanjim bhai casually named almost every single sticker at a glance.

![](https://res.cloudinary.com/b1tranger/image/upload/v1795712410/IMG_20261121_203138422_dvxf8r.webp)

Our discussions drifted across the current job market, the open-source ecosystem and how "AI slop" is flooding it, learning Linux from the ground up, and building in embedded systems. Tanjim bhai had just brought up Alan Kay and Carl Hewitt when we had to pause the deep dive—the main attraction of the afternoon had arrived: the cake.

![](https://res.cloudinary.com/b1tranger/image/upload/v1795712410/IMG_20261121_192031043_xj5dnk.webp)

Everyone grabbed a slice, gathered for a group photo, and hung around chatting afterward. Those of us from oU1TS had to head out a bit earlier than the rest since our final exams were underway, wrapping up an energizing afternoon off-screen.

* **Craft & Human Value:** Highlighted the human satisfaction of polishing work, language proficiency in prompt outputs, and why dozens of clones of the same idea (such as blood donation projects) fall flat without real collaboration.
* **Technical, Career & Learning Threads:**
* **CK Exam & Embedded Systems:** Discussed the CK exam in an ATS context and the career potential in embedded systems.
* *CK Exam (Certified Kubernetes Administrator / Application Developer):* Industry-recognized hands-on Linux Foundation certifications (CKA/CKAD) that act as strong keyword and skill signals for automated Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) in DevOps and cloud roles.
* *Embedded Systems:* Programming resource-constrained hardware and microcontrollers directly (often running stripped-down Linux or RTOS), offering strong engineering demand where hardware meets low-level software.


* **Pull Requests (PRs) & AI Context:** Question to revisit later on pull requests—what is the point when avoiding duplicate effort already done elsewhere? Also noted that AI scales action rather than replacing people, freeing humans to focus on what matters between people.
* **`Tux.bd` Portfolio:** Mentioned `Tux.bd` portfolio and its possible connection to `TuxTalk`.
* **Learning Linux from Scratch (Prompted by Ridaun bhai's question):** Remove the illusion that learning Linux has a fixed start and end; explore systems fundamentals through practical internals:
* *How Docker runs Linux:* Containers do not boot a full virtual machine; instead, they share the host's Linux kernel while isolating processes, filesystems, and networks using kernel features like **namespaces** and **cgroups**.
* *Alpine Linux:* A minimal, security-oriented Linux distribution built around **musl libc** and **BusyBox** instead of GNU coreutils, making it tiny (~5 MB base image) and ideal for understanding the bare essentials of a Linux userland and container environment.
* *LLVM:* A modular compiler and toolchain infrastructure that separates language frontends (like Clang for C/C++ or Rust) from target-specific backends via an Intermediate Representation (IR), offering insight into how source code compiles down to machine instructions.
* *Valgrind:* An instrumentation framework for dynamic binary analysis, primarily used to detect memory leaks, invalid memory accesses, and threading bugs at runtime in C/C++ programs.


* **Air-Gapped / Offline Software:** Software designed to operate on systems physically and logically isolated from the internet or external networks, requiring self-contained dependencies, local package mirrors, and offline verification/updates.
* **Alan Kay & Carl Hewitt:** Foundational computer scientists whose paradigms shape modern software architecture:
* *Alan Kay:* Pioneer of object-oriented programming (Smalltalk) and personal computing, emphasizing systems structured around independent objects communicating via **message passing** rather than shared mutable state.
* *Carl Hewitt:* Creator of the **Actor Model** of concurrent computation, where isolated "actors" are the fundamental primitives that process messages asynchronously without shared memory—a concept underlying modern distributed and fault-tolerant systems (such as Erlang/Elixir and Akka).

#### Security / Vulnerability Notes

* Quick notes touching on common attack surfaces and recon workflows:
* *Java-to-Shell Vectors:* Exploiting vulnerabilities in Java applications (such as insecure deserialization, JNDI injection, or arbitrary file upload in web containers like Tomcat) to achieve Remote Code Execution (RCE) and spawn an interactive system shell.
* *PHP Vulnerabilities:* Common web exploitation paths in legacy or misconfigured PHP applications, including Remote/Local File Inclusion (RFI/LFI), insecure file uploads, command injection, and SQL injection.
* *Printer Vulnerabilities:* Security flaws in networked print services and drivers (such as Windows Print Spooler / *PrintNightmare* or exposed IPP/SMB print ports) that attackers abuse for privilege escalation or lateral movement inside local networks.
* *SSH & Enumeration:* Using **enumeration** (systematically probing open ports, running services, user accounts, and misconfigurations) to map an attack surface, and leveraging **SSH** (Secure Shell) either as a target for credential/key discovery or as an encrypted tunnel/foothold once access is gained.
